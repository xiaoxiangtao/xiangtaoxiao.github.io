Notes on Requirement Analysis and Management

When you’re digging into what users actually need, it’s rarely something concrete and explicit. There’s the old Ford line — “if I’d asked people what they wanted, they’d have said a faster horse” — but once you get to actually building the product, you still have to land on something concrete: specific features that satisfy the underlying need. So what I mean by “requirement analysis and management” here is the whole chain: start from a surface-level ask, dig into the real need underneath it, evaluate it, land on a specific solution, and hand that off to engineers and designers to turn into an actual product.
